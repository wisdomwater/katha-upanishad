What happens when we stop running from death long enough to listen?

The Katha Upanishad begins with a young seeker named Nachiketa who finds himself at the house of Death. There he asks the question beneath so many of our fears: what, if anything, remains when the body dies?

Death answers slowly.

Through the image of the chariot, the distinction between the pleasant and the good, the sacred sound of Om, and the mystery of the Self hidden in the heart, Nachiketa is led inward toward a freedom that does not depend on escaping the changing world.

The Katha Upanishad: A 30-Day Contemplative Journey offers a modern devotional reading of this ancient text. Each day includes a short passage, a contemplative reflection, and a simple mantra for carrying the teaching into ordinary life.

Written from the perspective of a Christian contemplative approaching the Upanishad as a guest, this book does not try to erase the differences between traditions. Instead, it invites readers to listen deeply to another ancient voice and discover how its wisdom about desire, stillness, awareness, and impermanence may illuminate their own spiritual journey.

The book concludes with a complete modern rendering of the Katha Upanishad for continuous reading.

For anyone drawn to contemplative practice, interfaith wisdom, or the enduring question of what lies beneath the surface of the self, Nachiketa’s conversation with Death remains as alive as ever.

Sometimes the deepest answer is not found by looking farther away.

Sometimes we are invited to become still enough to notice what has been here all along.
