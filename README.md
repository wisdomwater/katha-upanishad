# The Katha Upanishad

## A 30-Day Contemplative Journey

This repository contains the source text for **The Katha Upanishad: A 30-Day Contemplative Journey** by Michael Collins.

The book is a modern contemplative reading of the *Katha Upanishad*, one of the ancient Upanishadic texts of India. It follows the story of Nachiketa, a young seeker who comes to the house of Yama, the lord of death, and asks one of humanity’s oldest questions:

**What remains when we die?**

Rather than answering with a simple description of the afterlife, Yama leads Nachiketa through a deeper inquiry into desire, discernment, the nature of the Self, stillness, awareness, and freedom.

## About the Book

The book is structured as a 30-day contemplative journey.

Each daily reading includes:

* a short passage from the *Katha Upanishad*
* a contemplative reflection
* a brief mantra for meditation or reflection

The readings move through six broad movements:

1. **The Question**
2. **The Choice**
3. **The Self**
4. **The Inner Journey**
5. **This Is That**
6. **Freedom**

The book also includes:

* a preface introducing the text and the author’s approach
* an epilogue reflecting on what it means to leave the house of Death
* a complete modern rendering of the *Katha Upanishad* for continuous reading

## Approach

This is not a scholarly commentary or a new academic translation of the *Katha Upanishad*.

The author approaches the text as a Christian contemplative and as a guest within another spiritual tradition. The goal is to listen carefully to the Upanishad on its own terms while allowing genuine resonances with contemplative Christianity and other forms of spiritual practice to emerge naturally.

The book intentionally avoids treating Hindu and Christian ideas as interchangeable. Terms such as **Ātman**, **Brahman**, **Om**, and **yoga** retain meanings rooted in their own traditions and should not be reduced to simple equivalents in Christian theology.

The guiding posture of the project is one of attentive encounter rather than synthesis.

## The Modern Rendering

The appendix contains a complete modern contemplative rendering of the *Katha Upanishad*.

It follows the traditional structure of:

* two major parts
* three vallis within each part
* six vallis total
* conventional verse numbering

The rendering is designed for clarity, readability, and contemplative use. It is not presented as a scholarly Sanskrit translation.

The wording was developed independently with reference to the Sanskrit text and older public-domain English translations, including the nineteenth-century translation of F. Max Müller.

## Major Themes

Among the central themes explored throughout the book are:

* the difference between the pleasant and the good
* desire and insatiability
* the chariot as an image of the inner life
* the Self hidden in the heart
* the limits of intellectual knowledge
* Om as a contemplative support
* the outward movement of the senses
* inward attention
* unity beneath apparent separation
* yoga as stillness and attentiveness
* the loosening of the knots of the heart
* freedom from the fear of death

One of the central refrains of the text is:

> **This, indeed, is That.**

The phrase expresses one of the deepest movements of the Upanishad: the reality we seek is not necessarily somewhere else. It is already present within the life, awareness, and mystery before us.

## Suggested Reading Practice

This book is designed to be read slowly.

A simple daily practice might be:

1. Read the passage once.
2. Read it again more slowly.
3. Sit with the contemplation.
4. Repeat the mantra several times.
5. Remain in silence for a few minutes.
6. Notice what stays with you during the day.

There is no need to force agreement with every philosophical claim. The invitation is simply to listen deeply and allow the text to ask its questions.
