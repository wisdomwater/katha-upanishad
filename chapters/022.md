# Day 22 — No Separation

> **2.1.10–15**
>
> What is here is also there; what is there is also here.
>
> One who sees only separation moves from death to death.
>
> This is known through the purified mind: there is no ultimate division here.
>
> One who sees reality as divided moves again and again through death.
>
> The Person, no larger than a thumb, dwells in the center of the heart and is Lord of what has been and what will be.
>
> Knowing this, one no longer shrinks away in fear.
>
> This, indeed, is That.
>
> The inner Person, luminous like a flame without smoke, is the same today and tomorrow, the same beyond time.
>
> This, indeed, is That.
>
> As rain falling on a mountain runs down in many separate streams, so one who sees only separate things runs after them in every direction.
>
> As pure water poured into pure water becomes one with it, so the Self of the awakened one rests in the One.

Yama now presses the teaching further. “What is here is also there; what is there is also here.” The distinction between near and far, inner and outer, sacred and ordinary begins to soften.

We are accustomed to experiencing life through difference. This is me and that is you. This is my body and that is the world. This is sacred and that is ordinary, this is life and that is death. Difference is real at the level of daily experience, but Yama asks whether difference is the deepest truth.

He says that one who sees only separation moves from death to death. This does not necessarily mean that distinctions disappear or that individuality is an illusion in some simplistic sense. Rather, the Upanishad is challenging the assumption that separateness is ultimate.

The image of rain on a mountain helps. Water falls as one rain, then divides into countless streams as it follows the contours of the land. From below, each stream may appear separate, but their source is the same.

Our lives can look this way too. We move through the world as distinct persons with different histories, bodies, desires, joys, and wounds. Yet the Upanishad asks whether beneath these differences there is a deeper unity, a shared ground of being from which all life arises.

Then comes the second image: pure water poured into pure water. Once joined, there is no meaningful boundary between one and the other. The water has not been destroyed; it has simply become impossible to say where one portion ends and another begins.

This is not an invitation to erase ourselves. The contemplative path does not ask us to pretend that relationships, responsibilities, or individual lives do not matter. It invites us to see individuality without making isolation the final word.

Much of our suffering is intensified by the sense that we are alone inside ourselves, separate from the world and from one another. Fear becomes sharper when life feels like something we must defend entirely on our own. The Upanishad offers another possibility: that beneath all the visible divisions, life belongs to a unity we have never actually left.

Perhaps this is why Yama says the inner Person is luminous like a smokeless flame. The same light appears in different lamps without becoming many different kinds of light. The forms vary, while the radiance itself remains one.

This teaching cannot be forced into an idea and then treated as solved. It asks to be lived with slowly. We may begin simply by noticing how often the mind divides reality into “me” and “not me,” “mine” and “theirs,” “inside” and “outside.”

Then perhaps, for a moment, we can loosen our grip on those boundaries.

The breath enters from the world and leaves us again. Water passes through the body and returns to the earth. Light enters the eyes, sound enters the ears, and relationship enters the heart. The border between self and world is already more porous than we usually imagine.

Yama is not asking Nachiketa to become less himself. He is inviting him to discover that the Self he seeks may be larger than the separate identity he has been defending.

What is here is also there.

What is there is also here.

**Mantra**

> **There is no ultimate separation here.**
